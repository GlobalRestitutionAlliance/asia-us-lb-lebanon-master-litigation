      Mandate and History

      UNRWA is the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East. The United Nations General Assembly established UNRWA in 1949 with a mandate to provide humanitarian assistance and protection to registered Palestine Refugees in the Agency’s area of operations pending a just and lasting solution to their plight.

      Principles and Values

      As an Agency with a humanitarian mandate – to assist and protect Palestine Refugees – humanitarian principles provide the space to make our work possible. From ensuring the sanctity of our shelters in times of crisis, to maintaining access to the most vulnerable populations, to advocating on behalf of Palestine Refugees, the principles of humanity, impartiality, neutrality, and independence are at the heart of all UNRWA operations.

      Leadership

      UNRWA is led by a Commissioner-General, appointed by the UN Secretary-General. The Commissioner-General is supported by a Deputy Commissioner-General for Programmes and Partnerships and a Deputy Commissioner-General for Operational Support.

Description

Lebanon continues being impacted by the consequences of multiple crises that have affected the country over the past years including the collapse of the economy and banking sector in 2019, the COVID pandemic and the Beirut Port blast in 2020, expanded armed conflict in 2024 and 2026, and years of political paralysis prior to 2025.

Around 490,000 Palestine Refugees are registered with UNRWA in Lebanon. As of September 2026, UNRWA has verified around 229,000 Palestine Refugees are living in Lebanon, including around 19,000 Palestine Refugees from Syria. They are among the most vulnerable communities in the country. The socio-economic crisis left over 83 per cent of Palestine Refugees living below the national poverty line, while unemployment exceeds 32 per cent, far higher than the national average[1].

The Agency’s operations in Lebanon are at risk due to chronic underfunding, while demand for services remains unchanged.

In Lebanon, the majority of UNRWA-registered Palestine Refugees are de facto stateless and without citizenship. They are excluded from all public services such as education, healthcare, and social security, and are unable to afford private sector services due to high poverty rates, which makes them highly dependent on UNRWA. UNRWA is also the sole provider of basic services such as road, water and sewerage network infrastructure including the collection and disposal of solid waste in the 12 Palestine Refugee camps in Lebanon.

Video Transcript:

Narrator:  Jonathan Fowler, Senior Communications Manager, UNRWA

"Here in Lebanon, Palestine Refugees are amongst the most vulnerable members of the population. 80 per cent of them live in poverty.

Without UNRWA they would lose their lifeline.  This is where UNRWA comes in.  UNRWA provides medical care for around 200,000 people a year.  37,000 students go to our schools.

UNRWA provides opportunities.  In twelve camps across the country, UNRWA also provides street sanitation, water, and other essentials for people to live a dignified life.

Without the support of the international community for UNRWA’s work, we risk the prospect of collapse of this community.

UNRWA needs to be able to keep working in Lebanon, as it does across the region."
